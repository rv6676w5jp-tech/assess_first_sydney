Sydney Town Hall House
456 Kent Street
Sydney NSW 2000

Re: Requesting a Formal Retention, Adaptation and Reuse Assessment Ahead of Demolition – Town Hall Square

Dear Lord Mayor and Councillors,

We are writing as architecture students from the University of Technology Sydney, currently developing an advocacy project on how Sydney assesses existing buildings before demolition is approved for major redevelopment. We are writing specifically regarding the Town Hall Square project.

We acknowledge that Town Hall Square is a significant addition to the city's public realm, and we are not writing to oppose the project's direction or ambition. Our concern is the process that has been undertaken, which defines most projects within Sydney. The seven buildings on the site are to be demolished on the basis that they are not heritage-listed and that upgrading them would cost more than the Council considers justifiable.

Our advocacy position is that existing buildings should be formally assessed for their capacity to be retained, adapted and reused. This is not a barrier to development, but a documented, evidentiary step against which any subsequent decision to demolish can be measured. We are developing a set of standards as one model of what this process could look like, alongside public-facing documentation intended to make this type of information more legible to the broader community.

We recognise that Town Hall Square is well advanced in its development stage, and we are therefore not asking Council to reconsider the project. We are asking whether the project team would be willing to indicate if any form of retention, adaptation or reuse assessment was undertaken for the seven buildings selected for demolition. We are also asking whether Council would consider formalising this method of assessment as standard practice for future redevelopment decisions.

We would welcome the opportunity to share the new standards framework we are developing, or to discuss this further with the project team at your convenience.

Yours sincerely,

Christopher FaresMaster of Architecture student, University of Technology Sydney
Damian ConnaughtonMaster of Architecture student, University of Technology Sydney
Vignesh SenthooranMaster of Architecture student, University of Technology Sydney
